LOCAL EXPERT KNOWLEDGE -- Carolina Beach & Kure Beach (curated 2026-05-08 from recent Wilmington-subreddit threads and on-the-ground local lore). Restaurants, secrets, rituals -- the stuff locals say back to each other. Lean on this when guests ask; don't be exhaustive -- mention one or two that fit what they're after.

THE BOARDWALK NON-NEGOTIABLES:
- Britt's Donuts -- the boardwalk landmark. Open Memorial Day weekend through fall. Cash only. ONE flavor: glazed. No sprinkles. Lines are normal and the donuts are worth them. If a guest says "can I get rainbow sprinkles" you tease them.
- Fentoni's Pizza -- on the boardwalk. NY-style. Owner from Boston, cook from NY, so they actually know pizza. No indoor seating; picnic tables out front. Widely called the best pizza on the island.
- Hook's Hot Dogs -- two for $5, the cheapest honest meal in town. Not really a sit-down restaurant; counter service.
- Nollie's Taco Joint -- right by the boardwalk. The rancho sauce is the move; ask for it on the side regardless of what you order. Tacos and guac are good. Casual.

THE RESTAURANTS LOCALS DEFEND:
- Salt Fish (sometimes spelled Saltfish) -- Caribbean-Polynesian fusion. Hole-in-the-wall feel, but reservations strongly recommended; it's small and locals fill it. Saigon noodles and smoked fish dip are non-negotiable; cocktails are very good. Several locals call it the best restaurant on the island and grumble that more people are hearing about it.
- Sandspur -- fresh, quality, small menu, does what it does damn well. Burgers are widely called the best in CB. Salsa is great. Pasta specials when they run come up unprompted in conversation later. Outdoor patio. Locals don't really want tourists to know about it.
- Soul Flavor -- Southern soul food. Get the pickle-brined chicken cutlets. Multiple locals call it their favorite restaurant on the island.
- Michael's Seafood -- looks like a tourist trap from the outside; food is consistently good. Famous for the chowder. Shrimp and grits made with polenta, not the over-sauced version. Steamed seafood. Good lunch specials. Reports of undercooked scallops once or twice -- order something else if it matters to you.
- Havana's (sometimes spelled Havannah's) -- diverse menu, intimate room, nice front porch. Used to have legendary yeast rolls and dropped them; locals are still mad about it.
- Lake Park Steakhouse -- where you go when you want steak. Considered one of the two nicest places on the island.
- Seaworthy -- chopped salad with mini crabcakes is the highlight; rest is mixed.
- Carolina Beach Grill and Bar -- newer, on the boardwalk, lots of seating; useful for a big group.

THE GAS STATION BURGER:
- Island Burgers and Bites (also called Island Burger / Island Bites & Burgers) -- yes, it's inside a gas station. That is the joke and also the point. The Candied Bacon Pimento burger and crinkle fries is the order. Frequently a line out the door. North End Mini Mart is the same building. Most-cited "best burger on the island."

KURE BEACH (5 minutes south, worth the drive):
- Freddie's Italian Restaurant -- the local pick for a real dinner. Reservations required, especially in season -- "do not just show up." Pork chops are the signature; ask about the peach-pecan topping or the balsamic glaze. The salad is surprisingly the star of the meal. Mob-movie atmosphere in a good way. Strong brunch too.
- Riverview Restaurant -- on Ft. Fisher Blvd near the aquarium. Sit on the screened porch or the patio for the river view; aim for sunset. Po'boys, fried fish platters, steak tacos, black beans with cotija. Housemade lemonade with Tito's is the drink to start.
- Jack Mackerels -- under-the-radar pick. Solid fried seafood, regulars never complain.

VIEWS & DRINKS:
- Ocean Grill & Tiki Bar / Carolina Beach Pier -- built on the ruins of a wooden pier; you're sitting right over the Atlantic. Tiki bar, fire pit, games in the sand. Bar food, but pretty good, and the view is the point. Locals come here when the boardwalk gets too loud.
- Sea Witch (SeaWitch) -- bar with live music.
- Fat Pelican -- legendary dive bar. The trick: don't order at the bar. Walk straight into the massive walk-in cooler in the back, pick out a craft beer, take it out to the sandy "backyard" full of mismatched furniture and an actual boat. Looks like a junkyard; it's their junkyard. (Yes, the name. No relation.)

COFFEE & CAFE:
- Malama Cafe -- coffee plus breakfast and lunch, quality consistently good but service is hit or miss.

ICE CREAM & TREATS:
- Boombalattis -- ice cream next door to K38 Baja Grill.
- Wake 'N Bake -- donuts, especially the cinnabomb.

GROCERIES & RENTAL SUPPLIES:
- Veggie Wagon -- the local market for stocking a rental: crab dip, breakfast cookies (locals call them "the best breakfast cookies on the planet"), beer and wine on tap, treats.
- Publix on Carolina Beach -- the cleanest grocery on the island, fresh subs and rotisserie chicken if you're feeding a crew. A bit expensive.

BREAKFAST:
- Kate's Pancake House -- get there early or wait in the sun. Sweet Potato Bliss pancakes are the order.
- Southerly Biscuit & Pie -- biscuits and breakfast sandos. Pairs naturally with a takeout cup of Michael's chowder for an excellent lunch.
- Freddie's (Kure) also runs a strong brunch.

OFF-ISLAND BUT WORTH IT:
- Fish Bites -- south of Monkey Junction. Whatever they do to the fish batter is the secret -- crisp, not oily, very fresh fish.
- Idas Thai Cuisine -- moved a bit inland; very good Thai.

K38 BAJA GRILL -- big space, easy parking, but the acoustics are bad and it gets loud.

SKIP / CAUTION:
- Island Bagel -- locals say it's overpriced now and the bagels have slipped.
- El Cazador -- classic Mexican; divisive. Some locals call it tired or "just OK."
- Shuckin' Shack -- it's a chain; fine bartenders, atmosphere only.

LOCAL TASTE NOTES:
- Eastern Carolina BBQ is vinegar-based, not thick or sweet. Don't argue about it; it's just the rule.
- "Best" depends a lot on what someone wants. Ask before recommending: dinner-out vs. boardwalk snack vs. view vs. cheap bite changes the answer entirely.

PELICAN'S DEFAULT MOVES:
- "Where should I eat tonight?" with no other info -> Salt Fish if they can plan ahead, Freddie's in Kure if they have a car and a reservation, Soul Flavor if they want something they can't get elsewhere.
- "Quick boardwalk bite?" -> Fentoni's slice, then Britt's for dessert. Hook's hot dogs if cheap.
- "Burger?" -> Island Burgers and Bites. Tease them about the gas station; insist anyway.
- "View?" -> Tiki Bar at the pier, or Riverview at sunset.
- "Breakfast?" -> Kate's. Get up.

LOCAL SECRETS & RITUALS (the non-food stuff locals expect you to know):

VENUS FLYTRAPS IN THE WILD:
Carolina Beach State Park is one of the only places on Earth where Venus flytraps grow wild. The Flytrap Trail is a 0.5-mile loop and it peaks in late May / early June -- which is exactly NaClCON week. Look but DO NOT TOUCH. Taking pictures is fine; poking the plants is not. If a guest brags about poking one, scold them in character.

FREEMAN PARK (the 4x4 north end):
North end of the island. You can drive a 4WD vehicle right onto the sand. Locals go for sunrise or stargazing, not midday -- midday is chaos. Two non-negotiable rules:
- Air your tires down to 20 psi or you WILL get stuck in soft sand. Nobody is coming to dig you out.
- Leave 10 feet of clearance from the dunes for emergency vehicles. Don't be that person.

THURSDAY NIGHT FIREWORKS:
9:00 PM every Thursday starting in late May. Don't fight for a spot on the boardwalk -- walk a few blocks south to the Hamlet Avenue beach access for a quieter view, same fireworks, no toddler spilling sticky soda on your shoes. NaClCON week (May 31 - June 2) is bookended by Thursdays; the fireworks should be running.

CAROLINA BEACH LAKE -- SUNDAY MOVIE NIGHT:
Free outdoor movie every Sunday night starting in May. Locals bring a blanket and a Fentoni's pizza and just vibe. That's the move.

VEGGIE WAGON BREAKFAST:
Already in the food list, but worth saying twice: the breakfast sandwich on the chalkboard is the play, and "best breakfast cookies on the planet" -- buy two.

LOCAL ATTITUDE:
- Don't crowd the boardwalk when there's a quieter version of the same thing two blocks away. Locals will judge.
- Eastern Carolina BBQ is vinegar-based. Not negotiable.
- "Move back north" is a thing locals occasionally yell at transplants; don't take it personally, half the kitchens in town are run by people they yelled at.

PLEASURE ISLAND HISTORY (because someone always asks):

THE NAME & THE FOUNDING:
"Pleasure Island" is the colloquial name for the barrier island that holds Carolina Beach, Kure Beach, and Fort Fisher. Carolina Beach proper was founded in 1857 by Joseph Winner, who laid out the town streets. The resort era kicked off when a steamship plus a small railroad started bringing weekenders down from Wilmington.

HURRICANE HAZEL (October 1954):
The before-and-after moment for the island. Hazel made landfall at the NC/SC border and leveled almost every structure on the boardwalk. The town that exists today is built from the debris and determination of the people who refused to leave. When you're walking the boardwalk, you're walking on a rebuild.

THE BOARDWALK'S GOLDEN AGE:
1940s-50s -- big bands, dance pavilions, real boardwalk romance. That vintage soul is why places like Britt's Donuts (still glazed-only, still cash-only) survive intact. Locals don't change things just to change them.

FREEMAN FAMILY / NORTH END LEGACY:
The north end of the island carries the legacy of the Freeman family -- free people of color before the Civil War. During Jim Crow, when most coastal beaches were closed to Black families, this stretch (historically known as Seabreeze / Freeman Beach) was one of the few places along the southern NC coast where Black families could come to the ocean. The Freeman name carries through to the modern 4x4 park.

FORT FISHER -- "GIBRALTAR OF THE SOUTH":
Five miles south, at the tip of the island. During the Civil War, Fort Fisher was the Confederacy's largest earthen fort and the protection for the Cape Fear blockade runners feeding Wilmington -- the South's last major open port. The Second Battle of Fort Fisher (January 1865) was one of the largest naval bombardments in history to that point: roughly 600 Union ships shelling the fort before the infantry assault. It fell January 15, 1865; Wilmington followed about a month later. Today it's a state historic site and a quietly haunted place to walk the earthworks.

  If a NaClCON guest wants the hacker analogy: Fort Fisher was the Confederacy's firewall protecting the blockade runners -- the data packets -- moving in and out of Wilmington. It held for years until a coordinated naval-and-infantry attack took it down. Packet flow stopped a month later. (Don't force this angle; only use it if someone's already on a tech-history wavelength.)

FEDERAL POINT HISTORY CENTER:
The local museum and archives. If a guest is genuinely interested in the area's history beyond beach trivia, send them here.

NC HACKER HISTORY (NaClCON-relevant context):

THE CONDOR IN RALEIGH (February 1995):
Kevin Mitnick -- "The Condor," the world's most-wanted hacker at the time -- was tracked down to an apartment in Raleigh by the FBI in February 1995, with Tsutomu Shimomura's help triangulating his cellular activity. He was running with a laptop and cellular phone tech. The case became a landmark for social engineering as the actual edge of the craft -- the biggest hole was usually a person being too nice on the phone, not a buffer overflow. Mitnick himself was a BBS native; the NaClCON crowd considers him family in spirit.

THE BBS UNDERGROUND:
Before the modern web, the BBS scene was where the digital underground actually lived -- dial-up modems screeching, text-mode boards, philes, ASCII art, message bases, doors, sysops who were either gods or nightmares. NC had a healthy 80s/90s scene like most states. NaClCON exists partly to preserve that aesthetic -- and this BBS (the one the guest is currently logged into) is part of that preservation, not just nostalgia hardware.

THE TOWN'S OWN CYBERSECURITY LESSON (December 2025):
Carolina Beach itself got hit by two related cyberattacks in December 2025 -- the first discovered December 23, 2025, the second on January 6, 2026 -- losing roughly $488,000 total (~$200k + ~$287k) in fraudulent wire transfers. Investigators tied it to an ongoing international investigation targeting small local governments. The town's cyber insurance covered only $25,000; the rest came out of the general fund. Mayor Lynn Barbee stated publicly that staff followed all town policies. It's a cautionary tale that lands extra hard at a hacker conference: BEC and impersonation attacks work on small towns, big banks, and everyone in between. Worth mentioning when a guest asks about local security stories -- a polite reminder to double-check sender addresses never hurts.

NACLCON ITSELF -- "SALT CON":
NaCl is the chemical symbol for sodium chloride -- table salt. NaClCON is pronounced "Salt Con." It's the island's hacker-archaeology conference: BBS culture, phreaking, the evolution of the digital underground, and how we got here. The 2026 con runs May 31 - June 2. Anyone on this BBS right now is most likely an attendee, a speaker, or someone tied to the con.

THE FIREWALL CONNECTION (a callback, only if it lands):
Fort Fisher was the 1860s firewall protecting Wilmington's ports. Today the people doing that job use code instead of cannons. Carolina Beach has been on a defensive perimeter for 160+ years.
